One algorithm, four machines

In 1981 Peter Struycken and two TU Delft engineers, F.C.A. Groen and P.W. Verbeek, turned a portrait of Queen Beatrix into a field of dots for the Dutch definitive stamp. This repository has re-implemented that procedure several times since May 2025, each time with a different generation of Claude. This page reconstructs who built what, what each version got right and wrong, and why the newest one is so large.

The procedure in two moves

1. Walk and pour. The photo becomes a grid of cells, usually 128 × 128, with darkness 0 (white) to 255 (black). A pen meanders through the rows, left to right and then right to left, adding each cell's darkness to a running sum. When the sum reaches 255 a dot drops into that cell and the remainder carries on. The source gives a worked example: 40 + 100 + 120 = 260, so the third cell gets a dot and 5 carries over.

2. Shake the box. The dots still sit on a visible lattice. Each dot owns a round territory. Dots are visited one by one, a die picks up, down, left or right, and the dot steps that way unless it would end up more than three places from where it started or enter another dot's territory. In that case it stays put ("dan moet hij maar op z'n plaats blijven"). Twenty passes melt the lattice into an even spray.

Source: Paul Hefting's account at kalden.home.xs4all.nl/stru. Every implementation below quotes it, some more carefully than others.

The implementations, one by one

Lens: I judge each version on two things, fidelity to Hefting's description and how well the engineering holds up. That lens misses aesthetics. An "unfaithful" shake can make a lovelier picture, and Struycken himself treated the procedure as material, not scripture.

index2, first version: the chat-era prototype

May 2025 · Claude 3.7 Sonnet (inferred) · commit f40a524 and follow-ups

Good

  • Phase 1 was right from day one: meander, carry, threshold at 255.
  • A spatial hash for the territory checks and a Fisher-Yates shuffle: real engineering, not toy code.
  • A thorough README that tells the history of the stamp, the analogy of marbles in a box, and the two-phase structure.
  • Within three days it gained a tone toolkit (brightness and contrast per range), zoom and pan, presets, and an overlay. That was enough to start playing with your own photos.

Bad

  • It read "at most three places" as the length of each step, not as a distance from the origin. Nothing records where a dot started, so over 20 passes a dot in a light area can drift far from its cell.
  • Moves go in random directions, rounded to whole cells, with five retries per dot. The source says one die roll and four directions.
  • The territory is a minimum distance of 2 cells, but phase 1 puts dots in adjacent cells, only 1 apart. Dense dark areas freeze on the lattice while sparse light areas wander. That is the opposite of what you want.
  • Phase 1 averaged RGB while the preview used luminance, so the dots disagreed with what you saw.
  • It used unseeded Math.random, so no result can be reproduced. The original's anti-counterfeiting idea depends on exactly that reproducibility.

index2, November patches: correcting it in small steps

Nov 2025 · Claude Sonnet 4.5 (inferred) · PRs #1 to #9

Good

  • It went back to the Dutch text and quoted it in a commit: "naar onderen of naar boven, of naar links en rechts". That gave four cardinal directions.
  • It moved step by step toward the faithful rule: cardinal moves, then exactly one cell, then 0.25-cell sub-steps with each dot tied to its origin.
  • It fixed the luminance mismatch and added useful workflow features: webcam capture, 4096 px export, a background gray for transparent PNGs, and a reset that keeps the image.
  • Small, scoped PRs with very clear commit messages. Easy to review.

Bad

  • Patch on patch: three different readings of "3 places" in one evening, each presented with confidence.
  • The meaning of the slider drifted: territoryRadius became movementStep, and in the code it acts as subgridOffset.
  • The bounds disagree. Dots are skipped at >= 3 cells, but the limit is enforced at 3 × subgridOffset.
  • It kept five attempts per dot and stayed unseeded, with no tests. Each fix answered your last complaint and nothing more.
  • "Modern (CS3+)" brightness is a plausible S-curve, not Photoshop's actual formula.

index3 and index4: the faithful reference

Jul 2026 · Claude Fable 5 · commits c0c86b4, 1b7417c

Good

  • A rewrite grounded in Hefting's text: one die roll per dot per pass, four directions, at most three sub-steps from the origin on each axis, and a move that breaks either rule leaves the dot where it is.
  • A DOM-free, UMD core (struycken-core.js) that runs in the browser and under node --test.
  • Deterministic: mulberry32, seeded with 1981 by default. A seed reproduces a pattern exactly and can be shared as a link.
  • 14 tests, including the source's own 40 + 100 + 120 example and a brute-force check of the territory rule. The same suite runs from a button in the page.
  • index4 reuses that engine for the everyday app, adding upload, drag-and-drop, paste, crop, presets, PNG and SVG export. It also waits for the webcam to report a frame size before capturing.

Bad

  • The text gives no territory size or subgrid, so 0.8 cells and 4 subdivisions are choices. They are sensible, but they are not "faithful".
  • Being strict makes it conservative: dots move at most ±0.75 cell, so with some settings traces of the lattice stay visible.
  • One fixed territory for every tone, as in 1983. Light areas look clumpy next to blue noise.
  • Two pages (reference and app) partly duplicate index2's UI plumbing.

Trix: the reinterpretation

Oct 2026 · Claude Opus 5.5 · 823e61c to ce3f9eb, live on GitHub Pages

Good

  • It names what the original is. Phase 1 is one member of a family of space-filling walks (Hilbert/gilbert, spiral, diagonal), close to Riemersma dithering. Phase 2 is hard-disk Metropolis Monte Carlo (1953). Once both are named, "Struycken 1983" becomes one recipe among twelve.
  • Integer arithmetic conserves ink exactly (dots × 255 + carry = total darkness), and a test proves it.
  • Territories follow the tone (a share of 1/√density). Steps are drawn as max · u², overlapping dots are allowed to relax apart, and step size can anneal. The README notes that with uniform steps the dark areas stayed on the grid. The tests caught this. That is the same failure index2 shipped with in 2025.
  • Steeped in stamp culture: perforation at the right gauge, Gerard Unger's lettering through a 30° gravure screen, the seed hidden in microtext, a postmark, and a sheet of sixteen OKLCH colors echoing Struycken's sixteen. A one-line TSP plotter tour nods to the PTT's Dr. Neher Laboratory.
  • Engineering with discipline: three inline scripts (core, render, app), Web Workers created from the script's own source so it works from file://, and 76 tests (unit, photo pipeline with contact sheets, headless Chrome with a fake webcam) that load the engine straight from the shipped HTML.
  • An independent review pass before release found real bugs: untrusted settings were not validated, SVG attributes were not escaped, stale workers were not killed, and a camera stream could arrive after the booth closed.

Bad

  • 6,100 lines in one HTML file. That fits the "self-contained" rule in CLAUDE.md, but it is hard to edit by hand.
  • A large surface means more to break. The final commits fix buttons that couldn't be clicked, overlapping labels and a layout that jumped, all things the first pass got wrong.
  • It departs from the original by default. The headline recipe uses a Hilbert walk and adaptive territories, and the 1983 procedure sits in second place.
  • Sound, a movie export and a shake-your-phone gesture are there to delight, not because the algorithm needs them. Whether that counts as scope creep depends on the brief (see below).

Why is the latest one so feature-rich?

Four reasons, roughly in order of weight:

1. The brief

The November 2025 sessions were steered by narrow asks. Branch names give it away: update-dotsize-slider, fix-dot-movement-algorithm. Each PR does exactly one thing. For Trix you challenged Claude to amaze you and to spend tokens freely. Neither commits nor README record the prompt, so this rests on your account, but the shape of the history fits. The engine came first, with 414 lines of tests, then 4,374 lines of interface in a single commit, then 13 rounds of polish, review and documentation in about three hours. An open brief permits breadth. A narrow brief caps it, whatever the model.

2. The foundation was already settled

index3 had already answered "what exactly did Delft do?" and pinned it down with tests. Trix didn't have to argue about fidelity again. It could treat 1983 as one recipe and spend its effort on generalizing.

3. Tests as a feedback loop, not decoration

May 2025 had no tests. July 2026 tested the algorithm. October 2026 tested the algorithm, real photos (with a tone-fidelity correlation and contact sheets to judge by eye) and the whole UI in headless Chrome. A model that can check its own output can keep building without the structure collapsing. That is what makes a 6,000-line single file workable.

4. Model generation and harness

From pasting chat output (2025) to short web sessions (late 2025) to long autonomous Claude Code sessions with a browser, Node and a review pass (2026): each step let the model work longer before it needed you. Opus 5.5 also brought domain knowledge into the code that earlier versions only quoted: Metropolis, Riemersma, rotogravure screens, Unger's lettering, and stamp gauges.

What carries forward

The constant across eighteen months is phase 1. Every version, including the earliest, got the meander and the carry right. Every misstep was in phase 2, which is where the prose is ambiguous ("3 plaatsen" of what?). Better models didn't just write more code. They read the source more carefully, said which parameters they had chosen themselves, and wrote tests that could catch their own misreadings. Trix's README admits that a test caught dark areas freezing on the grid. It's a small note, and the clearest sign of how far the work came from the version that shipped that same bug without noticing.
